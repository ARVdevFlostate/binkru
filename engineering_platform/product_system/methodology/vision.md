# Vision Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This methodology defines the recommended approach for developing, reviewing and maintaining a Product Vision within the Product System.

A Product Vision establishes the long-term purpose, direction and identity of a product. It serves as the primary source of product intent and provides strategic alignment for all subsequent product artifacts.

This methodology describes the process for creating a Product Vision. It does not define the structure of the Vision artifact, which is specified by the Vision Template.

---

# 2. Scope

This methodology applies to the creation, review and evolution of Product Vision artifacts governed by the Product System.

It is intended for:

- Product Owners
- Product Managers
- Product Leadership
- Product Contributors
- AI assistants supporting product development

---

# 3. Principles

A Product Vision should:

- express the long-term purpose of the product;
- establish a clear product direction;
- remain stable over time while allowing controlled evolution;
- communicate intent rather than implementation;
- guide strategic product decisions; and
- provide a foundation for all subsequent product planning.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

The Product Owner is responsible for:

- defining the Product Vision;
- ensuring the Vision reflects product intent;
- approving revisions; and
- maintaining the Vision throughout the product lifecycle.

Contributors may participate in developing the Vision.

AI may assist with drafting, refinement and analysis but does not approve or own the Product Vision.

---

# 5. Inputs

The Product Vision may be developed using information available at the time of creation.

## Required Inputs

There are no mandatory inputs prescribed by this methodology.

A Product Vision may be created whenever sufficient understanding exists to establish the long-term purpose, direction and identity of the product.

## Optional Inputs

Where available, the following inputs may assist in developing the Product Vision:

- business objectives;
- customer needs;
- market opportunities;
- organisational strategy;
- product constraints;
- stakeholder feedback; and
- lessons learned from existing products.

The relevance and availability of these inputs depend on the product context.

---

# 6. Method

The Product Vision should be developed through an iterative process.

Typical activities include:

1. Understanding the product context.
2. Identifying the product purpose.
3. Defining the intended customer value.
4. Establishing long-term product direction.
5. Reviewing the Vision with stakeholders.
6. Refining the Vision based on feedback.
7. Approving the Product Vision.

The methodology encourages iteration rather than sequential completion.

---

# 7. Review and Approval

A Product Vision should be reviewed for:

- strategic alignment;
- clarity;
- completeness;
- consistency with Product Principles; and
- long-term relevance.

The approved Vision becomes the authoritative expression of product intent.

---

# 8. Evolution

A Product Vision is expected to evolve over time.

Changes should occur deliberately through product governance.

Revisions should preserve continuity where appropriate while reflecting significant changes in product strategy or business direction.

Superseded versions should remain part of the product history.

---

# 9. Relationship to Other Artifacts

The Product Vision governs higher-level product intent.

Subsequent product artifacts derive from the Vision, including:

- Product Decision Records;
- Roadmaps;
- Milestone Plans;
- Release Plans; and
- Epics;

Changes to the Product Vision may require review of dependent artifacts.

---

# 10. AI Assistance

AI may assist in:

- exploring alternative Vision statements;
- analysing product context;
- identifying gaps or inconsistencies;
- improving clarity;
- validating against Product Principles; and
- preparing drafts for human review.

Strategic product intent remains human-defined.

Approval of the Product Vision remains a human responsibility.

---

# 11. Conformance

A Product Vision conforms to this methodology when:

- it follows the defined development process;
- it has an identified human owner;
- it has undergone appropriate review;
- it has been formally approved;
- it aligns with Product Principles; and
- it provides a stable foundation for subsequent product artifacts.
