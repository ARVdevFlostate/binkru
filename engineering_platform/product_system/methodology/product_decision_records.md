# Product Decision Record Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This methodology defines the recommended approach for developing, reviewing and maintaining a Product Decision Record within the Product System.

A Product Decision Record captures significant product decisions together with their rationale, expected outcomes and governance history. It provides traceability for decisions that influence the evolution of a product.

This methodology describes the process for creating a Product Decision Record. It does not define the structure of the artifact, which is specified by the Product Decision Record Template.

---

# 2. Scope

This methodology applies to the creation, review and evolution of Product Decision Records governed by the Product System.

It is intended for:

- Product Owners
- Product Managers
- Product Leadership
- Product Contributors
- AI assistants supporting product development

---

# 3. Principles

A Product Decision Record should:

- document significant product decisions;
- clearly describe the rationale for each decision;
- support traceability throughout the product lifecycle;
- remain factual and objective;
- preserve decision history; and
- provide context for subsequent product artifacts.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

The Product Owner is responsible for:

- approving significant product decisions;
- ensuring decisions align with the Product Vision;
- approving revisions; and
- maintaining the Product Decision Record throughout the product lifecycle.

Contributors may participate in analysing options, evaluating impacts and preparing decision proposals.

AI may assist with drafting, analysis and impact assessment but does not approve or own product decisions.

---

# 5. Inputs

A Product Decision Record may be developed using information available at the time of creation.

## Required Inputs

The following inputs are required:

- Product Vision

A Product Decision Record should always support or refine the established Product Vision.

## Optional Inputs

Where available, the following inputs may assist in developing a Product Decision Record:

- business objectives;
- customer research;
- stakeholder feedback;
- market analysis;
- technical constraints;
- regulatory considerations;
- previous Product Decision Records; and
- lessons learned.

The relevance and availability of these inputs depend on the product context.

---

# 6. Method

A Product Decision Record should be developed through an iterative process.

Typical activities include:

1. Identify the product decision.
2. Understand the decision context.
3. Evaluate available options.
4. Determine the preferred decision.
5. Document the rationale.
6. Assess expected impacts.
7. Review the decision with stakeholders.
8. Refine the decision based on feedback.
9. Approve the Product Decision Record.

The methodology encourages evidence-based decision making rather than subjective preference.

---

# 7. Review and Approval

A Product Decision Record should be reviewed for:

- alignment with the Product Vision;
- clarity of the decision;
- completeness of the rationale;
- consideration of impacts;
- consistency with previous product decisions; and
- governance compliance.

The approved Product Decision Record becomes the authoritative record of the decision.

---

# 8. Evolution

Product decisions may evolve over time.

Where a decision changes, the Product Decision Record should preserve the historical record while documenting the revised decision and its rationale.

Superseded decisions remain part of the product history.

---

# 9. Relationship to Other Artifacts

A Product Decision Record derives from the Product Vision.

Subsequent product artifacts may derive from Product Decision Records, including:

- Roadmaps;
- Milestone Plans;
- Release Plans; and
- Epics;

Multiple Product Decision Records may contribute to the same downstream artifact.

---

# 10. AI Assistance

AI may assist in:

- analysing available information;
- identifying alternative approaches;
- assessing decision impacts;
- identifying conflicts with existing decisions;
- improving clarity;
- preparing drafts for human review; and
- validating consistency across Product Decision Records.

Product decisions remain human-defined.

Approval of Product Decision Records remains a human responsibility.

---

# 11. Conformance

A Product Decision Record conforms to this methodology when:

- it follows the defined development process;
- it has an identified human owner;
- it is traceable to the Product Vision;
- it has undergone appropriate review;
- it has been formally approved; and
- it provides a governed record of a significant product decision.
