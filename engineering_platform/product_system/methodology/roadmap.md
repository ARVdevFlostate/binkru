# Roadmap Methodology

## 1. Purpose

This methodology defines the recommended approach for developing, reviewing and maintaining a Product Roadmap within the Product System.

A Product Roadmap communicates the planned strategic evolution of a product by organising approved Product Decisions into a sequence of initiatives. It provides direction for product planning while preserving flexibility as product understanding evolves.

This methodology describes the process for creating a Product Roadmap. It does not define the structure of the artifact, which is specified by the Roadmap Template.

---

# 2. Scope

This methodology applies to the creation, review and evolution of Product Roadmaps governed by the Product System.

It is intended for:

- Product Owners
- Product Managers
- Product Leadership
- Product Contributors
- AI assistants supporting product development

---

# 3. Principles

A Product Roadmap should:

- communicate strategic product direction;
- organise approved Product Decisions into coherent initiatives;
- prioritise customer and business value;
- remain adaptable to changing product understanding;
- avoid unnecessary implementation detail; and
- provide a foundation for subsequent planning artifacts.

---

# 4. Responsibilities

The Product Owner is responsible for:

- defining the Product Roadmap;
- ensuring alignment with the Product Vision;
- ensuring initiatives reflect approved Product Decisions;
- approving revisions; and
- maintaining the Product Roadmap throughout the product lifecycle.

Contributors may participate in prioritisation, sequencing and roadmap planning.

AI may assist with roadmap analysis, prioritisation, sequencing and impact assessment but does not determine product priorities or approve the Product Roadmap.

---

# 5. Inputs

A Product Roadmap may be developed using information available at the time of creation.

## Required Inputs

The following inputs are required:

- Product Vision
- Product Decision Records

The Product Roadmap should reflect the approved strategic direction and product decisions.

## Optional Inputs

Where available, the following inputs may assist in developing a Product Roadmap:

- business objectives;
- customer research;
- stakeholder feedback;
- market analysis;
- organisational priorities;
- product constraints;
- lessons learned; and
- previous Product Roadmaps.

The relevance and availability of these inputs depend on the product context.

---

# 6. Method

A Product Roadmap should be developed through an iterative process.

Typical activities include:

1. Review the Product Vision.
2. Review approved Product Decision Records.
3. Identify strategic initiatives.
4. Prioritise initiatives.
5. Determine initiative sequencing.
6. Review roadmap alignment.
7. Refine the roadmap based on feedback.
8. Approve the Product Roadmap.

The methodology encourages outcome-based planning rather than implementation-based planning.

---

# 7. Review and Approval

A Product Roadmap should be reviewed for:

- alignment with the Product Vision;
- consistency with approved Product Decision Records;
- strategic prioritisation;
- clarity;
- completeness;
- adaptability; and
- governance compliance.

The approved Product Roadmap becomes the authoritative strategic planning artifact for the product.

---

# 8. Evolution

A Product Roadmap is expected to evolve throughout the product lifecycle.

Changes should reflect approved Product Decisions, changing business priorities and improved product understanding.

Superseded roadmap versions remain part of the product history.

---

# 9. Relationship to Other Artifacts

A Product Roadmap derives from the Product Vision and approved Product Decision Records.

Subsequent product artifacts may derive from the Product Roadmap, including:

- Milestone Plans;
- Release Plans; and
- Epics;

The Product Roadmap communicates strategic direction rather than delivery commitments.

---

# 10. AI Assistance

AI may assist in:

- analysing Product Decision Records;
- identifying initiative groupings;
- recommending prioritisation;
- analysing dependencies;
- assessing roadmap impacts;
- identifying inconsistencies;
- preparing drafts for human review; and
- validating roadmap consistency.

Strategic product priorities remain human-defined.

Approval of the Product Roadmap remains a human responsibility.

---

# 11. Conformance

A Product Roadmap conforms to this methodology when:

- it follows the defined development process;
- it has an identified human owner;
- it is traceable to the Product Vision;
- it is traceable to approved Product Decision Records;
- it has undergone appropriate review;
- it has been formally approved; and
- it provides a governed strategic planning foundation for subsequent product artifacts.
