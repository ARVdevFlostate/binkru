# Collaboration Principles

## 1. Purpose

This document establishes the governing principles of the Collaboration System.

The Collaboration System governs cross-system interactions among two or more independent Engineering Systems where shared understanding, readiness, agreement, determination, coordination, or another collaborative outcome requires explicit governance.

These principles define the constraints that SHALL apply to Collaboration System domains, governed collaboration interactions, and their supporting mechanisms.

The Collaboration System SHALL preserve the semantic ownership, authority, lifecycle, and governance of participating systems.

---

## 2. Scope

These principles apply to all governed Collaboration System domains and interactions regardless of:

- the number or identity of participating systems;
- the collaboration purpose;
- the collaboration topology;
- the participating organizational roles;
- the operational workflow;
- the artifacts or representations used;
- the tools or technologies used; or
- whether activities are performed by humans, AI, automation, or a governed combination of participants.

These principles govern cross-system collaboration.

They do not redefine the authoritative semantics, responsibilities, authority, lifecycle, or governance of participating systems.

---

## 3. Collaboration System

The Collaboration System governs cross-system interaction where an authoritative collaborative outcome requires explicit governance.

A collaborative outcome may establish, where applicable:

- shared understanding;
- readiness;
- a governed agreement;
- a cross-system determination;
- acceptance or non-acceptance;
- eligibility;
- coordination;
- another explicitly governed collaborative outcome.

The Collaboration System owns governance of the applicable cross-system interaction and resulting collaborative outcome.

It does not thereby become the authoritative owner of the underlying domain semantics supplied by participating systems.

A collaborative outcome MAY be authoritative within its governed collaboration semantics without acquiring the authority of the participating systems whose authoritative inputs contributed to that outcome.

---

## 4. Collaboration Domains

A Collaboration Domain groups recurring cross-system interactions that share a meaningful collaboration boundary and warrant explicit governance.

A Collaboration Domain MAY involve two or more participating systems.

The number and identity of participating systems SHALL be determined by the governed collaboration need and SHALL NOT be constrained by structural convention.

A genuinely multilateral collaboration SHALL NOT be decomposed into bilateral interactions merely to conform to repository structure, naming convention, workflow tooling, or organizational convention where such decomposition would alter the actual collaboration semantics, authority, provenance, or governed outcome.

Conversely, independently governed interactions SHALL NOT be combined into a single multilateral collaboration merely because they involve overlapping systems.

Collaboration topology SHALL follow the governed semantics.

The existence of cross-system communication, dependency, or coordination does not by itself require a canonical Collaboration Domain.

A Collaboration Domain SHALL be introduced only where a distinct recurring cross-system interaction demonstrates an explicit governance need.

Domain names and repository structures SHALL reflect the governed collaboration need and SHALL NOT be required to encode the identities of all participating systems.

---

# 5. Collaboration Principles

## Principle 1 — Independent System Ownership

Each participating system retains ownership of its authoritative semantics.

Participation in collaboration SHALL NOT, merely through interaction:

- transfer semantic ownership;
- redefine authoritative meaning;
- broaden or narrow another system's semantics;
- override another system's authoritative state;
- manufacture missing authoritative semantics; or
- cause one participating system to acquire another participating system's authority.

Authoritative inputs used in collaboration SHALL remain attributable to their originating authoritative systems.

Collaborative consumption, composition, transformation, evaluation, or preservation of authoritative information does not transfer ownership of its semantics.

---

## Principle 2 — Governed Cross-System Interaction

Cross-system collaboration SHALL occur through a governed interaction where an authoritative collaborative outcome is required.

A governed interaction SHALL make sufficiently determinable, according to its applicable governance:

- its purpose;
- its participating systems;
- its applicable authoritative basis;
- its governed scope;
- its outcome; and
- the authority applicable to establishing that outcome.

Informal communication MAY support collaboration.

Informal communication SHALL NOT substitute for a governed interaction where applicable governance requires an authoritative collaborative outcome.

Not every cross-system interaction requires canonical Collaboration System governance.

---

## Principle 3 — Explicit Collaborative Outcomes

A governed collaborative outcome SHALL remain distinguishable from:

- the authoritative inputs used to establish it;
- participating-system states surrounding it;
- other independently governed collaborative outcomes;
- downstream decisions or states that may consume it; and
- operational activity used to establish or preserve it.

A collaborative outcome SHALL NOT silently establish another independently governed state merely because the states are related.

Where multiple governed determinations are required, they SHALL remain semantically distinct unless applicable governance explicitly establishes otherwise.

Absence of a governed collaborative outcome SHALL NOT be interpreted as a particular outcome unless applicable governance explicitly defines such semantics.

---

## Principle 4 — Shared Understanding

Collaboration SHALL establish sufficient shared understanding for the governed interaction being performed.

Shared understanding MAY include applicable:

- intent;
- scope;
- terminology;
- assumptions;
- constraints;
- dependencies;
- authoritative inputs;
- unresolved questions; and
- expected collaborative outcomes.

Shared understanding does not require participating systems to surrender their independent perspectives, responsibilities, semantics, or authority.

Agreement on shared context SHALL NOT be interpreted as transfer of semantic ownership.

Where material ambiguity or conflict prevents the applicable collaborative outcome from being authoritatively established, the ambiguity or conflict SHALL remain explicit rather than being silently resolved through assumption.

---

## Principle 5 — Authority Preservation

Responsibility to participate does not itself establish authority.

Technical capability does not itself establish authority.

Workflow permissions, system access, artifact authorship, organizational position, possession of relevant information, or ability to perform an activity do not themselves establish authority.

Authority Resolution MAY resolve applicable existing authority.

Authority Resolution SHALL NOT, merely through resolution:

- grant authority;
- broaden authority;
- aggregate independent authorities;
- transfer authority;
- manufacture authority; or
- exercise authority.

Where a collaborative outcome requires authority from multiple participating systems or governance domains, the applicable constituent authority requirements SHALL remain preserved.

The Collaboration System SHALL NOT acquire participating-system authority merely because it governs the interaction among those systems.

---

## Principle 6 — Responsibility and Accountability

Collaboration responsibilities SHALL be explicit enough to support the applicable governed interaction.

Responsibility for participating in, facilitating, coordinating, recording, validating, or executing collaboration does not itself establish authority over the corresponding governed outcome.

Accountability required by applicable governance SHALL remain identifiable.

Collaboration mechanisms SHALL NOT obscure responsibility, authority, or accountability by collapsing them into a generic notion of participation or approval.

Human, AI, and automated participation SHALL remain distinguishable where such distinction is material to applicable governance.

---

## Principle 7 — Traceability and Provenance

Material collaboration inputs, determinations, agreements, outcomes, and cross-system relationships SHALL remain sufficiently traceable and reconstructable according to applicable governance.

Authoritative inputs SHALL remain attributable to their originating authoritative systems.

Where a collaborative outcome depends upon authoritative inputs from multiple systems, the applicable basis and provenance SHALL remain sufficiently determinable.

Traceability does not imply semantic ownership transfer.

A collaboration mechanism SHALL NOT obscure the authoritative origin of material information merely by copying, composing, transforming, summarizing, evaluating, or recording it.

---

## Principle 8 — Progressive Refinement

Collaboration MAY progressively refine shared understanding, applicable context, agreements, determinations, or other collaborative outcomes where the governing interaction permits refinement.

Progressive refinement SHALL preserve the distinction between:

- authoritative source semantics;
- intermediate collaboration state;
- proposed collaborative outcomes; and
- authoritatively established collaborative outcomes.

Refinement SHALL NOT silently manufacture missing authoritative basis or convert an unresolved proposal into an authoritative outcome.

Material changes to applicable scope, authoritative inputs, conditions, or governing basis MAY require reassessment according to the applicable collaboration contract.

Where reassessment occurs, sufficient history and provenance SHALL be preserved to distinguish prior outcomes from subsequent outcomes.

A later determination SHALL NOT silently rewrite the historical basis or meaning of an earlier governed determination.

---

## Principle 9 — Transparency and Reconstructability

Governed collaboration SHALL be sufficiently transparent for applicable participants and authorities to understand the material basis, state, unresolved conditions, and outcome of the interaction.

Transparency does not require unrestricted disclosure of information where access is legitimately constrained by applicable governance.

Where information is restricted, the collaboration mechanism SHALL preserve sufficient governed indication of the dependency, restriction, or authoritative basis to support applicable decision-making and reconstruction.

Material collaborative outcomes SHALL be reconstructable from their applicable basis, provenance, authority, and governing context to the extent required by applicable governance.

Opaque tooling or automation SHALL NOT make a governed outcome authoritative merely because its internal operation cannot be inspected.

---

## Principle 10 — Structure Follows Governed Need

The structure used to govern a collaboration interaction SHALL follow its semantic and operational needs.

A Collaboration Domain or governed interaction MAY use:

- governing specifications;
- lifecycle documentation;
- templates;
- checklists;
- records;
- structured workflow representations; or
- other operational aids where warranted.

No lifecycle document, template, checklist, record, status model, approval artifact, or other structure SHALL be introduced merely to create symmetry with another Collaboration Domain or interaction.

A governed interaction MAY be fully specified without defining a separate collaboration lifecycle where no distinct lifecycle is semantically warranted.

Repository structure SHALL support governed semantics rather than determine them.

Artifact structure follows governed need.

---

## Principle 11 — Lifecycle Independence

A Collaboration System interaction MAY participate in the lifecycle progression of one or more participating systems without acquiring ownership of those lifecycles.

Collaboration SHALL NOT silently:

- merge independent system lifecycles;
- convert a collaborative outcome into a participating-system lifecycle state;
- establish downstream lifecycle authority;
- reinterpret an upstream authoritative state; or
- introduce mandatory sequencing where applicable governance does not require it.

Where a collaboration interaction has its own governed lifecycle, that lifecycle SHALL remain bounded to the collaboration semantics it governs.

Not every governed collaboration interaction requires a separate lifecycle.

---

## Principle 12 — Human, AI, and Automation Participation

Humans, AI, and automation MAY participate in governed collaboration interactions according to applicable governance.

Participation MAY include:

- retrieving authoritative context;
- resolving applicable sources;
- composing cross-system context;
- identifying ambiguity, gaps, or conflicts;
- evaluating explicitly established conditions;
- comparing authoritative inputs;
- preparing proposed collaborative outcomes or determinations;
- supporting validation;
- recording or preserving governed outcomes;
- maintaining traceability and provenance; and
- executing other explicitly governed collaboration activity.

Technical ability to perform an activity does not itself grant authority to establish the corresponding collaborative outcome or participating-system state.

AI or automation SHALL NOT manufacture missing authoritative semantics, authority, agreement, evidence, or determinations.

Where authority is explicitly delegated to automation, the delegation SHALL be:

- scoped;
- governed;
- traceable; and
- non-self-expanding.

Automation SHALL NOT infer broader authority from successful prior execution, technical permissions, workflow access, or possession of relevant information.

Human participation similarly does not manufacture authority where applicable authority has not otherwise been established.

---

## Principle 13 — Repeatability

Governed collaboration interactions SHOULD be repeatable to the degree appropriate to their semantics and context.

Repeatability means that materially equivalent authoritative inputs, governing conditions, scope, and authority can be processed through a sufficiently consistent governed interaction such that the basis and resulting outcome remain understandable and comparable.

Repeatability does not require identical outcomes where legitimate judgment, changed context, newly available authoritative information, or applicable authority produces a different determination.

Where materially equivalent interactions produce different outcomes, the material difference in basis, judgment, conditions, or authority SHOULD remain sufficiently explainable according to applicable governance.

Repeatability SHALL NOT be achieved by suppressing legitimate context, judgment, uncertainty, or participating-system independence.

---

## Principle 14 — Representation Neutrality

Collaboration semantics SHALL remain independent of specific:

- tools;
- vendors;
- workflow products;
- ticketing systems;
- source-control platforms;
- communication channels;
- document formats;
- approval mechanisms;
- AI models; or
- automation implementations.

Governed collaboration MAY be represented through suitable implementation mechanisms provided the applicable:

- semantic ownership;
- authority;
- interaction semantics;
- outcome distinctions;
- traceability;
- provenance; and
- governance obligations

remain preserved.

Technical representation SHALL NOT redefine the governed semantics it represents.

---

## 6. Relationship to Participating Systems

Participating systems remain independently governed before, during, and after Collaboration System interaction.

The Collaboration System MAY consume authoritative inputs from participating systems and MAY establish an authoritative collaborative outcome within the scope of the applicable collaboration contract.

It SHALL NOT thereby:

- acquire ownership of participating-system semantics;
- establish participating-system states outside the collaboration contract;
- redefine participating-system outcomes;
- manufacture participating-system authority; or
- supersede participating-system governance.

A collaborative outcome MAY become an authoritative input or condition for another governed interaction or participating-system process where applicable governance establishes that relationship.

Such dependency does not transfer ownership or collapse the independently governed semantics.

---

## 7. Evolution

The Collaboration System SHALL remain extensible.

New Collaboration Domains, governed interactions, specifications, lifecycle documentation, templates, checklists, records, or other operational mechanisms MAY be introduced where recurring collaboration demonstrates a distinct governance need.

The introduction of a new Collaboration Domain or interaction does not by itself require modification of these principles where the new collaboration conforms to the existing governing model.

These principles SHOULD be revised when the governing model of cross-system collaboration itself materially changes or when experience demonstrates that an existing principle is insufficient, contradictory, or no longer valid.

Evolution SHALL preserve applicable higher-order Engineering Platform architecture and governance.

Extensions SHALL NOT manufacture new authoritative system semantics merely for organizational convenience, implementation convenience, naming convention, or structural symmetry.

---

## 8. Conformance

A Collaboration System domain, governed interaction, or implementation conforms to these principles when it:

- preserves participating-system semantic ownership;
- governs authoritative collaborative outcomes through applicable cross-system interactions;
- supports the actual collaboration topology without imposing artificial participant cardinality;
- keeps collaborative outcomes distinct from authoritative inputs and independently governed states;
- preserves applicable responsibility, authority, and accountability;
- establishes sufficient shared understanding for the governed interaction;
- preserves required traceability and provenance;
- supports progressive refinement and reassessment without rewriting governed history;
- maintains sufficient transparency and reconstructability;
- introduces lifecycle and artifact structure only where warranted;
- preserves participating-system lifecycle independence;
- constrains human, AI, and automation participation according to applicable authority;
- supports appropriate repeatability without suppressing legitimate context or judgment;
- remains representation-neutral at the semantic level; and
- satisfies applicable higher-order Engineering Platform architecture and governance.

A technically functional collaboration mechanism is not conforming if it violates these semantic, authority, provenance, lifecycle, or governance obligations.
