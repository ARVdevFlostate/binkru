# Engineering Transition

## 1. Purpose

This lifecycle defines the governed process for introducing Product-approved Draft Epics from the Product System into the Collaboration System.

Engineering Transition establishes a shared understanding between Product and Engineering before collaborative Epic Refinement begins. It provides a structured collaboration through which participants review Product-approved Draft Epics, clarify business intent, identify engineering considerations, establish governed agreements and determine readiness to proceed.

Engineering Transition preserves Product ownership while enabling collaborative preparation for engineering refinement.

---

# 2. Scope

This lifecycle applies whenever Product-approved Draft Epics enter the Collaboration System.

It governs collaboration between the Product System and the Engineering System before collaborative Epic Refinement begins.

---

# 3. Objectives

The Engineering Transition aims to:

- establish a shared understanding of Product-approved Draft Epics;
- communicate the business intent behind the proposed Epics;
- identify engineering considerations, dependencies and assumptions;
- clarify responsibilities between Product and Engineering;
- establish readiness for collaborative Epic Refinement;
- record governed agreements established during the transition; and
- preserve end-to-end traceability.

---

# 4. Participants

Engineering Transition is a collaborative activity involving representatives from both systems.

Typical participants include:

**Product**

- Product Owner
- Product Manager
- Domain Experts (where appropriate)

**Engineering**

- Engineering Lead
- Solution Architect
- Technical Lead
- Engineering Contributors (where appropriate)

AI assistants may support preparation, analysis and documentation but do not establish governed agreements or assume accountability.

---

# 5. Entry Criteria

Engineering Transition may begin when:

- Product-approved Draft Epics are available;
- Product governance has been completed;
- supporting Product artifacts are available; and
- participating representatives have been identified.

---

# 6. Transition Activities

Typical Engineering Transition activities include:

1. Review Product-approved Draft Epics.
2. Review supporting Product artifacts where additional context is required.
3. Clarify business intent and expected outcomes.
4. Identify engineering assumptions.
5. Identify technical dependencies.
6. Identify significant risks.
7. Identify implementation considerations requiring collaborative refinement.
8. Record engineering observations.
9. Establish governed agreements.
10. Identify actions requiring further refinement.
11. Determine readiness for collaborative Epic Refinement.
12. Record the Engineering Transition outcome.

Engineering Transition establishes shared understanding and readiness for collaborative Epic Refinement. It does not perform engineering planning or implementation.

---

# 7. Exit Criteria

Engineering Transition is complete when:

- participants share a common understanding of the Product-approved Draft Epics;
- significant questions have been addressed or recorded;
- engineering observations have been documented;
- governed agreements have been recorded;
- required follow-up actions have been identified;
- the Engineering Transition Record has been completed; and
- the Epic Refinement lifecycle is ready to begin.

---

# 8. Outputs

Engineering Transition produces:

- an Engineering Transition Record;
- engineering observations;
- identified assumptions;
- identified dependencies;
- identified risks;
- governed agreements;
- agreed follow-up actions; and
- readiness for collaborative Epic Refinement.

---

# 9. Responsibilities

## Product

Product is responsible for:

- communicating business intent;
- clarifying Product-approved Draft Epics;
- answering Product-related questions;
- preserving approved business intent; and
- participating in governed agreements.

---

## Engineering

Engineering is responsible for:

- assessing Engineering considerations relevant to collaborative refinement;
- identifying technical considerations;
- identifying dependencies and risks;
- participating in governed agreements; and
- preparing for collaborative Epic Refinement.

---

## Shared Responsibilities

Product and Engineering jointly:

- establish shared understanding;
- preserve traceability;
- establish governed agreements;
- identify required refinements; and
- determine readiness to proceed.

---

# 10. Relationship to Other Lifecycles

Engineering Transition begins after Product approval of Draft Epics.

Successful completion of Engineering Transition enables the Epic Refinement lifecycle.

Engineering Transition does not authorise engineering implementation.

Engineering-ready Epics may enter the Engineering System for Engineering planning and governed realization. Engineering Transition does not establish Engineering implementation or realization authority.

---

# 11. Human and AI Collaboration

Humans remain responsible for:

- establishing governed agreements;
- clarifying business intent;
- agreeing collaborative outcomes; and
- accepting accountability.

AI may assist by:

- summarising Product artifacts;
- analysing dependencies;
- identifying inconsistencies;
- highlighting traceability;
- preparing Engineering Transition records; and
- recommending discussion topics.

AI does not establish governed agreements or assume accountability.

---

# 12. Conformance

An Engineering Transition conforms to this lifecycle when:

- entry criteria have been satisfied;
- required participants have been involved;
- collaboration activities have been completed;
- governed agreements have been recorded;
- exit criteria have been satisfied;
- traceability has been preserved; and
- the collaboration is ready to proceed to Epic Refinement.
