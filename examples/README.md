# Worked Examples

Worked examples show how the Engineering Operating Model can be applied in concrete Engineering situations.

They provide practical illustrations of how participants, authority, Engineering activity, evidence, automation, and governed outcomes can come together without replacing the canonical Engineering semantics that govern them.

> **Non-normative examples**
>
> Worked examples illustrate possible realizations of the Engineering Operating Model. They do not establish Engineering semantics or prescribe required implementations, processes, organizational structures, artifacts, tooling, or governance mechanisms.
>
> Where an example differs from the canonical Engineering Operating Model, the canonical model governs.

## Using the examples

Examples are organized by the Engineering perspective they are intended to illustrate.

Each example is independently understandable. You can begin with the category and example most relevant to what you want to understand; the examples do not form a required sequence, maturity model, or adoption path.

Concrete participants, tools, artifacts, workflows, and implementation mechanisms shown in an example are illustrative unless the canonical Engineering Operating Model establishes otherwise.

## Example categories

### Contextual Examples

Contextual examples show how the Engineering Operating Model can be realized in different organizational and participant contexts while preserving the applicable Engineering semantics.

They are useful for understanding how factors such as participant distribution, responsibility, authority, collaboration, evidence representation, and execution mechanisms may vary with context without creating different versions of the Engineering Operating Model.

[Explore the Contextual Examples](./contextual/README.md)

## Canonical Engineering Operating Model

The worked examples are an explanatory adoption surface. The authoritative Engineering semantics remain defined by the canonical Engineering Operating Model.

For the canonical architecture, specifications, Systems, Engineering Capabilities, and supporting Engineering semantics, see the [Engineering Platform](../engineering_platform/README.md).
