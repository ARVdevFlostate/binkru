# Contextual Examples

Contextual examples show how the Engineering Operating Model can be realized within different organizational and participant contexts.

They illustrate how participant distribution, responsibility, authority, collaboration, evidence representation, execution mechanisms, and governance ceremony may vary where the Engineering context warrants them while preserving the applicable Engineering semantics.

The contexts do not represent maturity levels, governance tiers, or progressively more complete implementations of the Engineering Operating Model. Organizational size and participant count do not independently determine Engineering rigor, evidence depth, or governance depth.

Each example is independently understandable and illustrates one possible realization. You do not need to read the examples in any particular order.

## Available examples

### Solo Developer

Shows the Engineering Operating Model in a context where one person may carry several distinct Product, Engineering, capability-evaluation, and Release capacities while AI and automation also participate in Engineering activity.

Concentrating responsibilities in one person can reduce coordination overhead without collapsing the semantic boundaries between those capacities.

[Read the Solo Developer example](./solo_developer.md)

### Startup Team

Shows the Engineering Operating Model in a context where Engineering activity, relevant responsibilities, and applicable authority are distributed across multiple participants.

The distribution of participants can require more explicit coordination, shared context, evidence representation, and continuity without creating different Engineering semantics or inherently requiring greater governance ceremony.

[Read the Startup Team example](./startup_team.md)

### Enterprise IT Team

Shows the Engineering Operating Model in a more organizationally distributed context where multiple teams or specialist participants may participate where applicable.

Greater organizational distribution can make authority, continuity, provenance, and coordination more explicit without making additional ceremony inherently desirable or required.

[Read the Enterprise IT Team example](./enterprise_it_team.md)

## Context changes realization, not semantics

The participant structure, responsibility distribution, authority distribution, collaboration topology, tooling, artifact representation, evidence representation, automation, and governance ceremony shown by a contextual example are characteristics of that realization.

They do not define context-specific versions of the Engineering Operating Model.

Applicable semantic ownership, authority, evidence, continuity, provenance, lifecycle, and conformance obligations remain governed by the canonical Engineering Operating Model.

For other kinds of worked examples as they become available, return to the [Worked Examples](../README.md). For the canonical architecture and Engineering semantics, see the [Engineering Platform](../../engineering_platform/README.md).
